Arabidopsis Piano

Sonifying Transcription

Transcription measurements reflects gene activity, and gene activity can be affected by external or internal stimulus. Yuchen Yang and colleagues (https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.RA120.013513) measured the effect of day/night cycle on each of the roughly 27,000 genes in Arabidopsis thaliana. This web app explores this experimental dataset uses *sound*.

You will learn a bit about the biology, as well as the science of sound and music theory.

By changing the frequency of the waveform to match the keyboard, the Synthesizer above actually throws away important experimental data. A subset of genes are naturally oscillating, and each have its own innate frequency. Here are two genes AT3G62250 (UBQ10) and AT5G61380(TOC1):

TOC1 is a known circadian gene and we can see the red line shows clear oscillating behavior that builds up during light and falls in the dark. UBQ10 on the other hand is a housekeeping gene that supports general cellular function. Compared to TOC1, UBQ10 does not show a prominant inherit rhythm. It would be more informative to explore the relative frequencies of genes that are naturally oscillating.

We can first identify which genes are naturall oscillating and then extract their oscillating frequency. The freqencies will be on the scale of a few cycles per day, but we can map them to a keyboard by scaling everything up into the audible range. This way, relative pitches between genes are mapped to their natural oscillation rates! Using musical harmony to explore relationship between oscillating genes is actually surprisingly appropriate. Human ears are really good at picking out certain mathmatical relationships between frequencies: The Pathagoreans supposedly discovered that whole number ratio relationships between freqencies sounds pleasant to our ears. In fact, the Pythagorean tuning maps each note in a major scale to have the following ratio relationships with the root (first) note:

1/1 9/8 5/4 4/3 3/2 5/3 15/8 2/1.

You can learn more about mathmatics of musical harmony here: https://www.asa3.org/ASA/education/teach/harmony-cr.htm https://aatishb.com/dissonance/ Two genes a perfect fifth apart (example here) suggest whenever one gene completes three cycles, the other one completes two cycles. While relationships discovered this way may not be biological (the methods sections discusses the limitations), they might be worth looking further into. At the very least, it is a fun way to explore this dataset and you can also use it to make some music!

Methods

Processing Raw Reads

The Diurnal Dataset from Yuchen Yang and colleagues is downloaded from GSE137732 . These are bulk RNAseq dataset from Arabidopsis grown under long-day conditions [16hr light / 8hr dark]

The paired end reads were processed with fastp with "--detect_adapter_for_pe". Results before and after trimming were checked using Multiqc. Reads were aligned to TAIR10 genome and transcripts were counted using salmon using the genome as decoy. DESeq2 was used to normalize the samples after using tximport to summarize transcript level data. Genes with less than 5 average reads across all samples were dropped.

UMAPs

Counts

Normalized counts were converted to log scale and z-score normalized before running UMAP with n_neighbors = 2, min_dist = 0.25, and metric = "cosine"

FFT

Each gene's normalized counts were interpolated using catmullrom, mean centered, and max-absolute scaled to between -1 and 1. FFT is performed on each waveform and trimmed to the first 15 freqencies, which is roughly where the magnitude drops to 0. Magnitude of all remaining frequencies are scaled relative to the strongest frequency thereby only preserving relative strength of each harmonics. Frequencies with magnitude 1000 fold weaker than the strongest frequnecies are scaled to zero. The resulting array of relative harmonic strength is used to generate the UMAP with n_neighbors = 2, min_dist = 0.25, and metric = "euclidean"

Oscillating Gene Detection

Lomb Scargle algorithm from astropy was used to detect inherit rythums of all genes with samples_per_peak = 30. Genes with False Alarm Probabilities (FAP) of larger than 0.35 was removed.

Gaussian Process Modeling

To capture a sense of confidence, the Gaussian Process was used to model the oscillating genes. gpflow was used to fit the GP model with a fixed period from Lomb Scargle and a Periodic SquaredExponential base kernel. Variance is set to the square of std, and mean is set to the mean of all measurements for the gene. Length scale of 6 was used as the default but additional fitting was done on length scales of 3 and 12 to see how much the fitted length scale changes. This shift is recorded as "sensitivity_ratio" which captures the robustness of the length scale from initial.
