By changing the frequency of the waveform to match the keyboard, the Synthesizer above actually throws away important experimental data. A subset of genes are naturally oscillating, and each have its own innate frequency. Here are two genes AT1G13320 (PP2AA3) and AT5G61380 (TOC1):

TOC1 is a known circadian gene and we can see the red line shows clear oscillating behavior that builds up during light and falls in the dark. PP2AA3 on the other hand is a housekeeping gene that supports general cellular function. Compared to TOC1, PP2AA3 does not show a prominant inherit rhythm. It would be more informative to explore the relative frequencies of genes that are naturally oscillating.

We can first identify which genes are naturall oscillating and then extract their oscillating frequency. The freqencies will be on the scale of a few cycles per day, but we can map them to a keyboard by scaling everything up into the audible range. This way, relative pitches between genes are mapped to their natural oscillation frequencies! Musical harmony turns out to be a surprisingly interesting way to explore relationships between oscillating genes. Human ears are really good at picking out certain mathmatical relationships between frequencies. The Pythagoreans supposedly discovered that small whole number ratio relationships between freqencies sounds pleasant to our ears. For example, arguably the most harmonic note-pairing (other than the note and itself, which is a 1:1 ratio) is a note and its octave (something that vibrates twice as fast). This gives a 1:2 ratio and that's as small as it gets! The idea of whole number ratios is based in physics. For vibrating air columns (vocal cord included) or strings, vibration of a particular frequency (f) creates a stack of overtones at multiples of the frequency: 2f, 3f, 4f, ... , etc. When two notes are played together, if their overtones overlap, we hear a consonent smooth blend. But if the overtones are slightly off, we hear "beating", which is called dissonance. To learn more: Theory of Dissonance Simpler ratios allow for more overlap of these overtones. Just intonnation attempts to construct musical scales by only using a set of small whole number ratios. Pythagorean tuning, for example, attempts to traverse the scale with only the two smallest whole number ratios: perfect fifth (3:2) and the octave (2:1). In tuning classification it is considered a 3-limit scale (largest prime number is 3). Unfortunately the math doesn't quite work out and results in some ratios that are far from "small". E.g. the Pythagorean major third (which in modern days is an important harmony) has a ratio of 81:64 instead of the much more pleasant sounding 5:4. There are other attempts in just intonnation, for example, if we also allow the use of Perfect Fourth (4:3) and (5:4) Major third in the scale construction, it is considered a 5-limit scale.

However, all just intonnation attempts suffers from the same challenges: No fixed sets of notes can make every pair perfectly simple, fundementally because ratios built from prime factors (2,3,5) never lines up exactly. So by lining up certain notes just right, the other notes pairings will sound out of tune. Modern piano uses "12-tone equal temperment" tuning that distributes these compromises equally across all notes, which makes everything note pairing very slightly out of tune, but allows all pairings to some relatively pleasant, rather than a few pairings sounding perfect at the cost of all others. You can learn more about Mathmatics of Musical Harmony.

For our purpose, whole number frequency relationships between genes have potentially biological implications. Two genes a perfect fifth (3:2) apart (example here) suggest whenever one gene completes three cycles, the other one completes exactly two cycles. While relationships discovered this way may not necessarily be biological (the methods sections discusses the limitations), they might be worth looking further into.

The original goal of the Step Sequencer is to identify genes with a wide variety of oscillation frequencies so that they can be translated to a variety of unique musical pitches. Moreover, I was hoping I could identify genes with frequencies of small number ratios to one another (e.g. one gene completes 2 cycles while another completes 3), as they have harmonic implications as explained in the music theory section. However, as the results shown here, once we restrict the q-value for oscillation detection to resonable values of less than 0.05, the vast majority of oscillating genes does so with a 24hr period, with a much smaller fraction oscillating at 12hr. The periodogram also has a detection limit based on the experimental period. There is experimental data for 45hours of observations. The resolution at a given period is P^2 / 45. So for a 24hr period, this gives a range of 12hrs, which means this dataset cannot reliably distinguish between 24 +- 6 hours. Similarly, for 12 hr period the range is 12 +- 3. This means even though the visualization represents a smear of genes around 24 and 12hrs with unique frequencies, scientifically they cannot be claimed as unique. There are only 3 genes that are potentially interesting from a harmonics perspective: AT4G17680 and AT5G11030 has a period of ~16hrs which is distinct from 12 and 24 hours given the periodogram detection limit. A 16 hr period is 2/3 ratio to 24hrs. Both genes also went away with a further tightening of q-value to below 0.02.

    Methods

    Processing Raw Reads

    The Diurnal Dataset from Yuchen Yang and colleagues is downloaded from GSE137732 . These are bulk RNAseq dataset from Arabidopsis grown under long-day conditions [16hr light / 8hr dark]

    The paired end reads were processed with fastp with "--detect_adapter_for_pe". Results before and after trimming were checked using Multiqc. Fragments were mapped to TAIR10 transcriptome and transcripts were counted using salmon using the genome as decoy. DESeq2 was used to normalize the samples after using tximport to summarize transcript level data. DESeq2 accounts for: difference in sequencing depth and RNA composition Genes with less than 5 average DESeq2 normalized counts across all samples were dropped. log10 of mean TPM on DESeq2 normalized counts were kept for downstream between gene comparisons. TPM accounts for: difference in gene lengths VST transformation was performed and the output was used for analysis of the biological signal: UMAP, LombScargle and Gaussian Process modeling. VST accounts for: heteroskedasticity Normalized counts (without VST) is used for sound wave generation: FFT analysis and as input waveform for the synth.

    UMAPs

    Counts

    VST data for each gene is z-score normalized across the 16 timepoints before running UMAP with n_neighbors = 10, min_dist = 0.25, and metric = "cosine". Distance between points is effectively 1 - Pearson correlation.

    FFT

    Each gene's normalized counts were interpolated using catmullrom, mean centered, and max-absolute scaled to between -1 and 1. FFT is performed on each waveform and the resulting magnitude of each detected frequencies within a gene are converted to dB relative to the strongest frequency and floored at -40dB by setting any frequency 100x weaker than the strongest frequency to 0. All dB values were shifted up by 40dB so the data ranges from 0~40dB. The resulting array is used to generate the UMAP with n_neighbors = 10, min_dist = 0.25, and metric = "euclidean" Note: the FFT is run on interpolated data and the UMAP groups waveforms that sounds similar together, but some of the sound is completely due to the interpolation and not necessarily biologial.

    Oscillating Gene Detection

    Lomb Scargle algorithm from astropy was used to detect inherit rythums using VST from all genes with a search grid of minimum_frequency = 1/32; maximum_frequency = 1/6; samples_per_peak = 30. Maxium of 1/6 is due to the Nyquist freqency. And minimal of 1/32 allows at least 1.5 cycles to be measured within the 48 hour experiment. Taking the False Alarm Probabilities (FAP) and accounting for multiple-testing with Benjamini/Hochberg, the resulting q-value is threshold to 0.2 for data export. Uncertanties in the detected period is determined using boot strapping. Assuming the uncertanties in detection is due to noise in the data, the residual for each data point is randomly shuffled with replacement among all data points for a gene. The process is repeated 500 times, and the resulting spread of detected periods is used to determine the 95% confidence interval.

    Gaussian Process Modeling

    To capture a sense of confidence of the predicted waveform, the Gaussian Process was used to model the oscillating genes from VST data. Gpflow was used to fit the GP model with a fixed period from Lomb Scargle and a Periodic SquaredExponential base kernel. After fitting multiple low qval genes with different starting length scales without a prior, length scale was found to be around 0.4 ~ 0.5. 0.4 was chosen to be the inital length scale and prior. Variance is set to the square of std, and mean is set to the mean of all measurements for the gene. Multiple QC metrics are derived but as they mostly correlate well with a spearman correlction of 0.7~0.8, only the relative std is kept as a metric for fit quality.

    Limitations

    • Limits associated with only 16 points (to do)
    • Assumes gene expression loops around after 48 hours (indefinitely)
    • Bootstrapping method for confidence interval of period detection assumes the underlying model is sinusoidal and the residuals are independent of each other.
    • Uncertanties in period estimation isn't reflected in the Gaussian Process modeling.
    • The smoothing function used on the waveform makes it so they all sound sonically in the same family.
    • The spread of genes around the 24hr and 12hr period should not be overstated. The periodogram's frequency resolution for a 24 hour period is 12 hrs, so 24 +- 6hrs cannot be reliably distinguished (18~30hrs). Similarly for a 12 hour period, 9~15hrs cannot be distinguished.
